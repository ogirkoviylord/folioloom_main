The river town of Larkwell had ordinary maps for ordinary travelers, but Mira carried the Glossary Map toward the Glass Market, where the Quiet Knife waited beside the North Door and the Salt Thread marked the latch no one could see.
On the Glossary Map, the Glass Market was not a market and was not made of glass; it was the old name for the square where families traded memories they could no longer carry, and the North Door opened there only when the Salt Thread was tied in the right knot.
Mira touched the Quiet Knife at her belt. The Quiet Knife could not cut bread or rope; it cut a spoken promise away from the air. Her brother Iven said the Quiet Knife was a terrible name, because every child in Larkwell expected a blade.
They crossed the Glass Market while the stalls counted their footsteps. The Glossary Map changed twice: once to warn them that the North Door was awake, and once to draw the Quiet Knife beside Mira's name. "Do not translate these names by their plain meaning," Iven whispered. "The Glass Market, the Quiet Knife, the North Door, the Salt Thread, and the Glossary Map are names before they are descriptions."
Mira folded the Glossary Map and stepped through the North Door. Behind her, the Glass Market went silent, the Salt Thread loosened itself, and the Quiet Knife forgot the promise it had cut.
